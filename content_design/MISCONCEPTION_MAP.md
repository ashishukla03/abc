## 1. Overview & Theoretical Framework

Learners do not arrive at STEM activities with empty minds; they arrive with firmly held **naive intuitive mental models** (Carey, 2000; Driver et al., 1994). When learners encounter unexpected phenomena without targeted cognitive scaffolding, they frequently distort their observations to fit their existing misconceptions rather than revising their fundamental beliefs.

> [!NOTE]
> Prevalence descriptions and intuitive mental models identified in this document represent pedagogical working hypotheses grounded in cognitive development and science education literature (Carey, 2000; Driver et al., 1994; White & Gunstone, 1992), rather than verified empirical baseline statistics from a localized student cohort.

In Gramin PocketLab, every subject micro-lab diagnoses and explicitly refutes at least three prevalent domain misconceptions using the **Predict-Observe-Explain (POE)** paradigm (White & Gunstone, 1992).

## 2. Subject-by-Subject Misconception Diagnostic Table

| Subject | Misconception Code & Title | Naive Student Belief / Intuition | Diagnostic Action or Trigger in Lab | Targeted Cognitive Scaffolding Script | Cognitive Mechanism for Resolution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mathematics** | **MATH-M1: Raw Ratio Confusion** | Learner thinks ratio numbers ($1:2:3$) represent the actual physical units to distribute ($1\text{ L}, 2\text{ L}, 3\text{ L}$). | Enters $1, 2, 3$ in steppers, leaving 6 units unused in tank. | *"1 + 2 + 3 uses only 6 units in total. Look at the water tank—6 units are still left over! Ratios show proportional shares, not the final liter amounts."* | Contrast observation with system invariant (unused resource remainder). |
| **Mathematics** | **MATH-M2: Additive Equipartition** | Learner believes sharing among three parties always means equal parts ($12 \div 3 = 4, 4, 4$). | Allocates $4, 4, 4$ across plots A, B, and C. | *"You gave 4 units to each plot. That is an equal 1 : 1 : 1 ratio. But Plot B needs twice as much as Plot A, and Plot C needs three times as much!"* | Highlighting violation of stated inequality constraints ($B = 2A$). |
| **Mathematics** | **MATH-M3: Arbitrary Sum Match** | Learner believes any three numbers that sum to 12 satisfy the condition (e.g. $3, 4, 5$). | Allocates $3, 4, 5$, hitting total 12. | *"3 + 4 + 5 equals 12, but look closely: 4 is not double 3! In a 1 : 2 : 3 ratio, each share must multiply the base unit by 1, 2, and 3."* | Multiplicative comparison check ($4 / 3 \ne 2$). |
| **Biology** | **BIO-M1: Arrow Flow Reversal** | Learner thinks arrows in food webs point to what an animal hunts or eats. | Draws or interprets arrows as `"Frog hunts Grasshopper"`. | *"In science food webs, arrows show where the food and energy travel. When a frog eats a grasshopper, energy moves from the grasshopper into the frog: Grasshopper $\rightarrow$ Frog."* | Redefining arrow semantic label to `"Energy transfers to"`. |
| **Biology** | **BIO-M2: Direct-Only Relationship Blindness** | Learner believes predator loss only affects its direct prey; ignores indirect trophic effects. | Predicts grasshoppers will not change when snakes are removed. | *"In this simplified farm food web model, look at the chain reaction! When snakes vanish, frog numbers face less predation and tend to increase. As more frogs feed on grasshoppers, grasshopper numbers tend to decline. Ecosystems are interconnected!"* | Step-by-step intermediate node highlighting in a simplified qualitative ecological model ($A \downarrow \implies B \uparrow \implies C \downarrow$). |
| **Biology** | **BIO-M3: Apex Predator Superiority** | Learner believes carnivores/predators are the foundation of ecosystem stability. | Devalues grass/crops, assuming predators sustain the food chain. | *"Without sunlight and green plants, no energy enters the web at all. If paddy crops die from drought, herbivores starve, and all predators collapse."* | Ecological energy pyramid grounding. |
| **Chemistry** | **CHEM-M1: Neutrality Implies Absolute Purity** | Learner believes water with $\text{pH} = 7.0$ is sterile, germ-free, and guaranteed safe to drink. | Assumes a clear water sample (e.g. untreated well or pond water) testing neutral at pH 7.0 requires no further purification. | *"Caution! Neutral pH only means hydrogen and hydroxide ions balance each other out ($H^+ \approx OH^-$). A pH reading cannot detect microscopic bacteria, viruses, or chemical toxins. Water safety testing requires biological and chemical assays separate from pH; boiling disinfects pathogens but does not eliminate chemical or heavy metal contaminants."* | Explicit dissociation of chemical acidity from biological contamination and toxic chemical hazards. |
| **Chemistry** | **CHEM-M2: Scale Value Inversion** | Learner assumes higher numbers on the pH scale mean stronger acids. | Categorizes $\text{pH } 10$ as acidic. | *"Remember the scale direction! Numbers lower than 7 are acids (lower number = stronger acid). Numbers higher than 7 are bases. 7 is neutral."* | Visual anchored number line with directional color coding. |
| **Chemistry** | **CHEM-M3: Indicator Neutralization Fallacy** | Learner believes dipping an indicator strip chemically purifies or alters the solution. | Expects an indicator paper strip to turn acid into neutral water. | *"Indicators do not clean or change the liquid. They are just chemical detectors that change color to report whether acid or base is present."* | Functional tool categorization (detector vs. reactant). |
| **Physics** | **PHYS-M1: Loudness Equals Pitch** | Learner believes louder sounds have higher frequency. | Increases amplitude and expects the tone to sound like a flute. | *"Listen carefully and look at the wave! Increasing amplitude makes the sound louder and the wave taller, but the number of vibrations per second (pitch) stays the same."* | Visual decoupling on canvas (height vs. horizontal peak compression). |
| **Physics** | **PHYS-M2: Pitch Alters Travel Velocity** | Learner believes high-frequency screeching travels faster through the air than deep thuds. | Chooses "travels twice as fast" on prediction question. | *"All sound waves travel through the air at the exact same speed (~340 meters per second). Frequency changes how fast the air vibrates back and forth, not how fast sound crosses the room!"* | Physical medium constant explanation ($v = f \cdot \lambda$). |
| **Physics** | **PHYS-M3: Wave Height Equals Frequency** | Learner associates wave height (vertical dimension) with frequency (horizontal dimension). | Confuses vertical amplitude with frequency on oscilloscope canvas. | *"Wave height shows Amplitude (how much energy and loudness). Wave packing (how close the peaks are together horizontally) shows Frequency!"* | Direct coordinate axis visual annotation ($X = \text{Time}$, $Y = \text{Displacement}$). |
